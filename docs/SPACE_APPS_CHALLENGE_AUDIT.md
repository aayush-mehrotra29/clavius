# Clavius and the lunar south-pole planning challenge

## Verdict

Clavius supports the core decision in the supplied brief for **Connecting Ridge and the Shackleton rim**. A user can compare dates at both sites, inspect the Sun and Earth against each local horizon, choose a feasible transfer, place a rover route, and see the outcome in one mission report. The strongest evidence concerns reproducibility and astronomical directions. Terrain-dependent crossing times, actual radio service, rover traction and ease of use by first-time users have much weaker evidence.

## Coverage of the brief

The balanced score favors the weaker of normalized incident energy and Earth contact. Each site/date screens 72, 96 and 120 hour transfers. | “Best” means the highest-ranked feasible member of that finite search. The flight model assumes a freely oriented Earth parking orbit and a prescribed descent. Launch-pad access, propulsion sizing and fuel reserves need separate models. |

## What the NASA data actually drive

- **Terrain and visibility.** PGDA 78 supplies nominal 5 m LOLA elevation and uncertainty/support products. PGDA 90 supplies the 80 m regional terrain for distant horizon obstructions. The planning search uses the nominal realization. A 5 m posting cannot resolve a wheel-scale rock. [NASA local products](https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/78) · [NASA regional product](https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/90)
- **Sun, Earth and time.** The pinned NAIF kernels supply DE421 geometry, lunar orientation, frame and time conversion. Archived JPL Horizons DE441 vectors independently check 32 site/body/date directions across 2000–2049. Maximum angular differences were 0.0000092° for the Sun and 0.0000105° for Earth, within the 0.001° test threshold. This check isolates astronomical geometry. [NAIF kernels](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/naif/data_generic.html) · [JPL Horizons](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/) · [Direction checks](../public/data/horizons-geometry.json)
- **Imagery and relief.** LROC stereo orthoimages at 1 m/pixel and stereo terrain at 2 m posting supply the detailed planning display at each site. The surface physics uses the LOLA grid. The global Moon Kit supplies overview imagery, while the dated PGDA 90 NAC images are reserved for shadow validation. Product identities, resolutions, hashes and roles are recorded separately. [Traverse imagery catalogue](../public/data/drive/traverse-relief.json) · [Dataset inventory](NASA_DATASETS.md)
- **Integrity.** The local verification checked 14 terrain assets, five kernel assets, each site’s three stereo inputs and both rendered relief/slope layers against their pinned hashes. The reviewed mission’s saved flight hash matched the traverse reference, the selected transfer hash matched its planner candidate, and the recorded site terrain and kernel hashes agreed across all three stages. ## Decision check using a saved mission

With a level panel and a 24-hour window, moving the Shackleton landing from **8 to 15 January 2028** changed modeled incident energy from **146.41 to 65.25 Wh/m²** and Earth visibility from **24 to 0 hours**. On 15 January the Sun remained above the terrain throughout the window. A sunlit panel can still receive little energy when sunlight strikes it at a grazing angle. In the selected traverse, the hypothetical R1 drove **6.74 m** and reached **one of three waypoints** before the 180 s timeout. The requested science dwell remained unfinished.

To reproduce the comparison, start the landing search on 1 January 2028 at 00:00 UTC with seven-day spacing, a 24-hour window, a level panel and balanced priority. A repeat on the running local service took **4.66 s** including polling and reproduced all six candidate records and the ranking. This is one warm-service measurement.

## Remaining validation work

1. **Establish timing error at the terrain horizon.** In the observed local LROC comparison, the 95th-percentile shadow-boundary discrepancy was 200 m at Connecting Ridge and 283 m at Shackleton on a 200 m evaluation lattice. Only one observed epoch supported both local sites. One other scene had insufficient supported rays. Several numerical refinement gates remain open. Window crossings interpolate between 10-minute samples through 72 hours and 30-minute samples for longer windows, which can miss brief events. Validate rise/set times against independent site-specific observations before attaching an operational timing tolerance. [Illumination validation](ILLUMINATION_VALIDATION.md)
2. **Measure comprehension.** Give first-time users a schedule-slip exercise and record time to a defensible choice, whether they distinguish sunlight hours from incident energy, and whether they interpret zero direct Earth visibility correctly. The existing timing result measures the solver and service only.
3. **Qualify downstream predictions separately.** The active R1 uses assumed rigid-ground friction and power properties. Report its recorded outcomes as simulation results. Wheel/soil targets, VIPER hardware reconciliation and battery calibration remain open.
