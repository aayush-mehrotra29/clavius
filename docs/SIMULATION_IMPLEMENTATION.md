## Design reference lock

The current priority is a small library of documented NASA robot models and stronger environment/physics validation, following the user's 15 November direction. Broad schematic/import support is a future extension; the existing bounded URDF importer remains available. Clavius R1 is hypothetical and must not be relabeled as NASA flight hardware. Each added model needs exact hardware sources, missing-property assumptions and a stated validation domain.

The existing Clavius dark workspace owns typography, selection color and layout. The workflow adapts Isaac Sim's import/inspection pattern, Aerie activities/resources, Foxglove linked-time replay/charts, and NASA Trek's layer/coverage inspection. Reference links are in README. The lunar scene uses measured elevations at true 1× height; relief, slope and height-RMS colors are inspection layers, not new measurements.

## Flight to surface replay

The default workspace at `/app` submits a dated flight and uses the saved flight ID to start the surface job. Flight inputs select a UTC departure from a freely oriented 200 km circular Earth parking orbit, one of the two nominal PGDA78 site origins and 72–120 hours to approach. The solver targets a 50,000 km Moon-centered patch 18 hours before approach and a point 100 km radially above the site in the rotating DE421 mean-Earth frame. Each short-way Lambert coast uses a point-mass force law. A Cartesian DOP853 integration checks the endpoints to 10 m and checks central-body apsides against reference radii. At the patch Clavius budgets an explicit velocity correction. The braking impulse at approach starts a prescribed 900 s radial descent whose height reaches zero with zero body-fixed speed by construction. The solver reports the required maneuver budget. Launch-site access, tankage, thrust limits, third-body perturbations and touchdown dispersion remain open. The existing GMAT reference campaigns and their pending acceptance gates retain their separate scope.

The surface API takes the stored site's nominal ENU origin and modeled touchdown UTC directly from the flight. MuJoCo drives the selected physical rover with wheel-velocity feedback on actual chassis position and heading. It attempts up to five waypoints in a continuous 180 s simulation and requires uninterrupted presence within 0.18 m during the final task. The terminal event, contact, attitude, joint, velocity and energy samples come from this run. Power integrates the native motor/base energy accumulated at every physics step plus any active science load, using the declared array, native attitude and DE421 Sun direction with local terrain horizons at roughly 5 s intervals. Battery depletion stops the simulation at the first depleted telemetry sample. No thermal or deformable-soil model runs in this workflow. Surface illumination includes display fill. The R1 surface finishes and wheel braces are cosmetic. Its collision geometry, mass and inertia come from its hypothetical URDF.

The browser moves from the global Moon to the matching nominal PGDA78/PGDA90 display terrain and plays the saved ENU poses, joint angles and trace. White dashes mark the requested route and a solid line shows traveled positions. A failed route remains available to inspect and edit. Flight and traverse IDs reopen local results. Public API regression checks cover dated trajectories, landing handoff, native travel, dwell completion and rejected overrides in `backend/tests/test_expedition.py`.
