# Why simulate a rover traverse at the lunar south pole?

**Working claim.** A route and a departure time are one planning decision for a solar-powered rover. A team comparing two plans needs to know *when* a ridge will block the Sun, *which activity* the rover will be carrying out, and *how much reserve* its battery model predicts at that point. Clavius lets a team rehearse that decision at Connecting Ridge. NASA’s VIPER planners describe the same coupling of time, position, science and engineering constraints. [VIPER mission planners’ conference paper](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230004239/downloads/SSR2023-VIPER-Planning-Ennico.pdf)

## Reading order and decisions

1. **Identify the decision a planner makes.** Read Ennico-Smith et al., *VIPER Mission Traverse Planning – Design, Strategies, and Dynamics* (2023 conference contribution). Extract an example of a strategic plan revised at the tactical level, the constraint that prompts the change, and who needs to see it. Use it to storyboard a science stop that shifts the rest of the traverse. Treat VIPER’s plans as the mission team’s design evidence. [NASA manuscript](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230004239/downloads/SSR2023-VIPER-Planning-Ennico.pdf)
2. **Find the closest existing approach.** Read Speyerer et al., *Optimized traverse planning for future polar prospectors based on lunar topography* (2016). R-Traverse already connects terrain, illumination and energy. Record its route objective, vehicle assumptions, spatial scale and modeled outputs. Compare that with Clavius’s user-defined route, departure-time comparison and replay. A published optimum belongs to the vehicle and scenario that produced it. [Icarus paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2016.03.011)
3. **Establish why the chosen site and timing matter.** Read Gläser et al., *Illumination conditions at the lunar south pole using high resolution Digital Terrain Models from LOLA* (2014). Extract the Connecting Ridge coordinates, study interval, observer height and longest continuous dark interval wherever reported. The longest dark stretch is the quantity to compare with the battery’s endurance. Two sites with similar annual sunlight fractions can still demand different reserves. [Icarus paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2014.08.013)
4. **Document the terrain under the demo.** Read Barker et al., *Improved LOLA elevation maps for south pole landing sites* (2021), alongside PGDA Product 78. Examine the illumination analysis in Figure 14 and distinguish grid spacing, interpolated cells and height uncertainty. Record which files correspond to Site01. Use that source card beside the relief graphic and identify wheel-scale terrain and regolith behavior as open questions. [Barker et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pss.2020.105119) · [NASA product and file descriptions](https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/78)
5. **Find an observation that could challenge the model.** Read Speyerer & Robinson, *Persistently illuminated regions at the lunar poles* (2013), and inspect the LROC WAC south-pole sequence. Select individual source images with identifiable acquisition times and coverage at Connecting Ridge. The time-weighted product uses gap filling, so look up each original frame before comparing an epoch. Use these images as independent evaluation material. [Icarus paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2012.10.010) · [WAC sequence](https://data.lroc.im-ldi.com/lroc/view_rdr/WAC_POLE_MOVIE_SOUTH) · [Time-weighted product notes](https://data.lroc.im-ldi.com/lroc/view_rdr/WAC_POLE_ILL_TWI_SOUTH)
6. **Explain the scientific reason for going.** Read Li et al., *Direct evidence of surface exposed water ice in the lunar polar regions* (2018). Extract how the authors identified patchy exposures and why an orbiter detection still leaves questions for a surface mission. Use one sentence to motivate polar prospecting. A claim about ice along a particular Clavius route would require colocated evidence. [Open paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6130389/)

## The first three pieces of Clavius evidence to produce

| Question | Procedure | Deliverable and interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does departure time change this decision? | Choose one Connecting Ridge traverse with one science stop. Fix the waypoints, speed, vehicle, initial charge, reserve threshold, panel model, loads and observer height. Choose three departure times in advance, spanning a modeled lighting transition. Export every run, including any that look alike. | One plot of battery reserve versus elapsed time with shadow intervals overlaid. Tabulate minimum reserve in Wh, minutes in shadow and the first threshold crossing, or “none.” Describe this as a sensitivity result for this example. |
| Does the modeled shadow occur where the camera saw it? | Select dated LROC frames before inspecting predictions. Match location, UTC, frame and surface-level observer height. Compare at the camera’s effective spatial scale. Mask missing, ambiguous and mixed pixels. Include both a sunlit interior and a shadow edge. | One observation/prediction overlay per epoch, a lit/shadow confusion table, and a discrepancy map. Report sample sizes and offsets. If mismatches concentrate on an edge, investigate registration, footprint and horizon sampling before claiming agreement. |
| Does the tool help an actual user? | Ask rover designers or robotics students to choose a departure and explain the first limiting event, once with a terrain map and a table of the same inputs and once with Clavius. Counterbalance task order with equivalent scenarios. | Record time, correctness against the fixed scenario, and the reason given. Publish counts and anonymized examples, including mistakes. Use measured results to decide whether a claim about quicker or clearer planning is warranted. |

For each source card, record the research question, exact page or figure, finding in the team’s own words, study conditions, what could contradict it, and the design decision it informed. A practical first session is to complete cards 1–3, sketch the paired battery plot, and select the three departure times before running it. The next session can tackle the independent image comparison.

Use the hero to name the capability. Put a mission-team citation beside the challenge, a source citation beside the terrain graphic, and measured comparisons in the benchmark section once available. Local gravity/driving benchmarks and the measured-terrain probe exercise specific engine behavior. Shadow agreement, battery prediction against telemetry, and the benefit to users each need their own evidence.
